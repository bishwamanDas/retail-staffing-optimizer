# ADR 0002: First-Difference OLS for Controlled Marginal Productivity Estimation

## Status
Accepted

## Context
When assessing whether to replace a departing retail employee, business owners typically divide total revenue by current headcount (yielding average sales per employee, approx. Rs. 12.93 L in the dataset).

Using average sales commits the **marginal productivity fallacy**:
1. Diminishing returns: each additional staff member adds less incremental output than existing senior staff.
2. Confounding: headcount often expands during peak holiday demand periods, falsely attributing seasonal sales surges to the extra employee.

## Decision
We estimate marginal productivity using a **first-difference Ordinary Least Squares (OLS)** regression:
$$\Delta \text{Sales}_t = \beta \cdot \Delta \text{Employees}_t + \sum \gamma_m \cdot D_{m,t} + \epsilon_t$$

Where:
- $\Delta \text{Sales}_t = \text{Sales}_t - \text{Sales}_{t-1}$
- Differencing eliminates the store's secular trend and unobserved time-invariant store characteristics.
- Calendar indicator dummies $D_{m,t}$ control for cyclical seasonal spikes.
- $\hat{\beta}$ captures the true marginal revenue generated by adding one employee (Rs. 11.25 L).

## Consequences
- Produces realistic break-even and payback estimates.
- Prevents overestimating projected ROI from additional headcount.
